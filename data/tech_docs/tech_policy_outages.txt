TECH POLICY: INCIDENT MANAGEMENT AND OUTAGES

1. Definition of Incident
An incident is any unplanned interruption or quality reduction of the SaaS product or internal systems.
Incidents are categorized by severity: SEV1 (critical), SEV2 (major), SEV3 (minor).

2. SEV1 Incidents
SEV1 incidents include full-service outages, data loss, or security breaches affecting multiple customers.
SEV1 requires immediate response from on-call engineers and incident commander activation.
Status updates must be posted to the status page at least every 30 minutes.

3. SEV2 and SEV3 Incidents
SEV2 incidents are partial outages or performance degradation impacting a subset of customers.
SEV3 incidents are issues with limited impact or easy workarounds.
For SEV2 and SEV3, updates should be posted at intervals appropriate to the impact.

4. Communication
During an incident, all customer communication must follow the approved templates in the incident communication runbook.
Engineers must avoid sharing internal-only information or speculating about root cause.

5. Post-Incident Review
All SEV1 and SEV2 incidents require a post-incident review within 5 business days.
Reviews must include a timeline, root cause analysis, and a list of corrective actions with owners and due dates.

6. Incident Ownership
Every incident must have a clearly identified incident commander responsible for coordination.
The incident commander is accountable for assigning roles, tracking actions, and ensuring updates are communicated.
Ownership may be handed over between shifts, but this must be documented in the incident record.

7. Incident Channels and Tools
All real-time incident collaboration should take place in the designated incident chat channel.
Incident tickets must be created in the tracking system with an appropriate severity and impacted services.
Engineers should keep notes in the incident ticket or a shared document for later review.

8. Customer-Facing Notifications
Customer status updates must avoid revealing sensitive internal details and focus on impact, mitigation, and next steps.
Templates for acknowledgments, updates, and resolution announcements are available in the communication runbook.
All customer communications during severe incidents should be reviewed by support leadership or communications.

9. Change Freeze During Incidents
During active SEV1 incidents, non-essential deployments and configuration changes should be paused.
Any emergency change must be documented and rolled back if it worsens the situation.
Once the incident is resolved, normal change processes can resume.

10. Continuous Improvement
Incident metrics such as time to detect, time to mitigate, and time to resolve should be monitored.
Patterns of recurring incidents should trigger deeper root cause analysis or architectural reviews.
The incident management policy will be refined over time based on lessons learned.
