HR POLICY: PARENTAL LEAVE, BIRTH, AND ADOPTION

1. Eligibility
Parental leave is available to all employees with at least 6 months of continuous service.
This policy covers birth, adoption, or fostering of a child under the age of 16.
Local legislation may provide additional rights which supersede this policy.

2. Maternity and Birthing Parent Leave
Birthing parents are entitled to 16 weeks of paid leave at 100% of base salary.
Leave can start up to 4 weeks before the expected due date with a medical certificate.
Extensions may be granted in cases of medical complications or multiple births.

3. Partner and Paternity Leave
Non-birthing partners are entitled to 4 weeks of paid leave at 100% of base salary.
Leave may be taken in one continuous block or split into up to two periods within the first 6 months.
Partners should coordinate with their manager to ensure adequate business coverage.

4. Adoption and Foster Care
For adoption or long-term foster care, primary caregivers are entitled to 12 weeks of paid leave.
Secondary caregivers are entitled to 4 weeks of paid leave.
Documentation must be provided to HR as required by local law.

5. Requesting Parental Leave
Employees must notify HR and their manager as soon as practicable, ideally 12 weeks before the start date.
Requests should include expected dates, whether the employee is the primary or secondary caregiver, and any planned split of leave.
HR will confirm eligibility, pay, and benefits in writing.

6. Benefits During Parental Leave
During parental leave, employees continue to accrue PTO in accordance with local regulations.
Health insurance and other benefits generally remain active, subject to plan rules and applicable laws.
Employees should contact HR for a summary of benefits that continue while on leave.

7. Job Protection and Return to Work
Upon returning from parental leave, employees will typically be reinstated to the same or a comparable position.
Any proposed changes to role, reporting line, or location must be discussed with the employee and HR in advance.
Managers should schedule a return-to-work meeting to align on priorities and any flexible arrangements.

8. Flexible Work After Parental Leave
Employees may request flexible work arrangements when returning from parental leave, such as reduced hours or remote work.
Requests should be made in writing, outlining the preferred schedule and duration.
Managers and HR will review such requests considering both employee needs and business requirements.

9. Multiple Parental Leave Events
Employees may become eligible for parental leave more than once during their employment.
Each qualifying event (birth, adoption, or foster placement) is evaluated independently under the prevailing policy and legislation.
HR will confirm entitlements in each case, including any impact on seniority or benefits.

10. Confidentiality and Support
Information shared with HR about pregnancy, fertility treatment, adoption, or fostering is treated as confidential.
Employees are encouraged to reach out to HR if they need additional support, such as counseling resources or employee assistance programs.
HR may update this policy over time, and employees will be informed of material changes.

During parental leave, employees continue to accrue PTO and remain eligible for benefits.
For complex cases, employees should schedule a confidential consultation with HR.
